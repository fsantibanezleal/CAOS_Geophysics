## M07. Topography-aware electrical resistivity tomography

### Governing physics and inverse

For current electrodes A and B, the quasi-static DC potential u in a conductive earth obeys

$$
\nabla\cdot(\sigma(\mathbf r)\nabla u(\mathbf r))
=-I[\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r_A)-\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r_B)],
\qquad \rho(\mathbf r)=1/\sigma(\mathbf r).
$$

Here σ is conductivity in S/m, ρ resistivity in Ω m, I injected current in A, and the delta terms locate the source and sink. For potential electrodes M and N, transfer resistance is R=(u_M-u_N)/I in Ω. The geometric factor k has units m; apparent resistivity is ρa=kR in Ω m. For a collinear flat homogeneous half-space,

$$
k_{\rm flat}=\frac{2\pi}{r_{AM}^{-1}-r_{AN}^{-1}-r_{BM}^{-1}+r_{BN}^{-1}},
$$

where signed electrode separations r are in m and electrode order must be preserved. This is an analytic sign and scale oracle. Slagdump's relief requires a numerical topographic geometric factor. Replacing it by kflat changes the forward model.

The discrete inverse solves for positive resistivity in a topography-following 2D parameter mesh using the pinned pyGIMLi ERT manager and documented fixed regularization. For validation, the fitted model predicts held-out resistance using the same surveyed geometry and topographic factors. The reported score is log-resistance RMSE against a training-only homogeneous baseline. The receipt also retains signed residuals, mesh hashes, stopping state, Jacobian sensitivity/coverage, and predeclared mesh and assumed-error perturbations.

### Assumptions, eligibility and negative controls

The pinned provider object is a Wenner-type .ohm survey with 38 sensors and 222 quadrupoles. The parser requires its explicit grammar, finite local x/z positions, valid ABMN indices, nonzero dipole lengths and valid resistance. Coordinates are interpreted as local metres with z positive upward; there is no geographic CRS or vertical datum. No implicit units, silent deduplication, invalid-row dropping or guessed columns are permitted.

No reciprocal pairs and no instrument-error column are supplied. Implemented weighting assumes 3% relative error plus a 0.001 Ω absolute floor. Conditional chi-square under those assumptions is not measured instrument precision. QC outliers are flags, not deletions or robust reweights. Invalid factor oracle, nonpositive data, inverse nonconvergence, or either blocked validation failure must remain ineligible, unverified or not-converged.

The numeric gates include the four-electrode half-space formula, pyGIMLi flat-factor agreement, a refined homogeneous half-space forward control, topographic factor contrast, field holdout prediction, receipt identity and failure retention. Both the deterministic interleaved and central spatial-block splits are required. A good interleaved result alone is insufficient because quadrupoles share electrodes and survey conditions.

## M08. Instrument-corrected waveform processing and classical onset candidates

### Observation and processing equations

For channel c, integer samples d_c[n] in counts are modeled as a channel response to projected ground motion q_c, plus trend and noise:

$$
d_c[n]\simeq(h_c*q_c)(t_n)+b_c(t_n)+\eta_c[n],
\qquad q_c=\mathbf a_c^\mathsf T\mathbf u.
$$

Here h_c is the complete instrument/acquisition response in counts per native unit, q_c is displacement in m, velocity in m/s, or acceleration in m/s² as declared, b_c is offset/trend and ηc is unmodeled noise. The orientation vector ac comes from StationXML azimuth and dip in the documented frame. Response epoch, rate, timing, units and channel identity must match. Raw counts are never assumed to be velocity.

The workflow detrends a declared window, applies a recorded taper, evaluates the full analogue/digital response H(f), and applies a stabilized inverse in a declared prefilter band:

$$
\widehat Q(f)=D(f)T(f)G(f),
$$

where D is the tapered data spectrum, T is the prefilter, and G is the inverse response with a declared water-level floor where |H| is small. It then applies an acausal, zero-phase SOS bandpass to a private array and computes windowed Welch PSD with density scaling. PSD units are the square of its input physical unit per Hz. The result is a conditional band-limited motion estimate, not a new instrument calibration.

There are two distinct classical onset lanes in this repository. The protected waveform workflow in waveform_processing.py applies ObsPy classic STA/LTA to each admitted channel and stores unclassified trigger intervals with phase=null. The matched M08 comparator used in the locked M08/M13 study is implemented separately in phase_picking.py: it forms three-component energy e[n]=Σc x_c[n]² and computes a short-to-long moving-energy ratio:

$$
C[n]=\frac{N_s^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N_s-1}e[n-j]}
{\max(N_l^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N_l-1}e[n-j],\epsilon)}.
$$

For the matched comparator, xc is normalized channel data, Ns and Nl are sample counts corresponding to 0.12 s and 1.2 s at interval Δt, and ε prevents division by zero. Its first upward crossing of the configured threshold (default 2.5) is the first onset candidate; a later crossing at least 0.4 s later is provisionally labeled S. In the protected workflow, channel-specific characteristic functions use the request's STA/LTA lengths and on/off/refractory thresholds, and candidates remain phase=null. Neither rule is a phase classifier. The second trigger can be coda/noise and weak phases can be missed. No analyst label selects a window or candidate.

### Eligibility, negative controls, rights and status

Eligible processing requires exact MiniSEED records and StationXML epochs/response chain, a declared UTC window and explicit parameters. Gaps, overlaps, malformed clocks, clipping, unsupported response stages, missing/ambiguous epochs, invalid units, flat signals or too-short valid windows fail or remain QC-only. No alternate channel, silent resampling, response guessing or zero-filled gap is permitted. Optional catalogue evaluation runs only after the candidate arrays have been sealed; reference matching is deterministic, one-to-one and retains misses and unmatched candidates.

Implementation is substantial across local science and protected user/API/UI layers. Current design and tasks still list actual native/runtime and host qualification as separate open gates. Ordinary tests and authored controls do not establish instrument field accuracy, Linux/Windows resource limits or complete release acceptance. Source data rights are source-specific. User-provided bytes need owner authority; file format support does not grant redistribution rights.

## M09. First-arrival travel-time tomography

### Governing equation and inverse

For isotropic speed v(r), first-arrival travel time T(r) satisfies the eikonal equation away from a source:

$$
|\nabla T(\mathbf r)|=s(\mathbf r)=1/v(\mathbf r).
$$

T is in s, coordinates in m, v in m/s and slowness s in s/m. In ray form, pick i is

$$
t_i=\int_{\Gamma_i}s(\mathbf r)\,d\ell ,
$$

where Γi is the fastest path between surveyed source and receiver and depends on the velocity model. In a homogeneous half-space, t=||rg-rs||/v supplies an independent geometric oracle. The actual M09 uses pyGIMLi's Dijkstra travel-time forward operator and regularized nonlinear inversion on a parameter mesh.

The field workflow weights picks using σt=max(0.03t, 0.0001 s), explicitly an assumed error rule because the source file supplies no errors. It withholds shot groups, fits a training-only homogeneous slowness baseline for each split, predicts the frozen holdout without refitting, and reports signed residuals and summed ray-path-length coverage. Both an interleaved shot split and a central spatial-block split must improve over their own baselines by at least 10%; at least two of three held shots must improve on each split and the engine must stop normally before its iteration limit. Start-model and finer-mesh sensitivity are retained.

### Eligibility and limits

The strict reader requires exact shot/geophone grammar and finite positions, valid IDs, positive times and no undeclared trailer. Source units are interpreted as provider-based x/y metres and t seconds; the file itself does not declare CRS, local coordinate units, vertical datum or pick uncertainty. Local profile coordinates are not geographic elevation. QC flags anomalies without deleting observations. No reciprocal pairs exist, so reciprocal precision is unavailable.

The independent homogeneous oracle checks analytic time, reciprocity and speed scaling against a separate Dijkstra mesh. A failed oracle, source mismatch, solver failure, failed split or receipt drift must not be called a successful velocity result. Coverage is raypath length, not a resolution kernel. The convergence record reports fresh-process repeatability after fixing the inner CGLS controls, but this does not establish unique geology or transfer to another site.

## M10. Acoustic full-waveform inversion

### Wave physics and objective

The controlled engine models 2D constant-density scalar acoustics in the Deepwave sign convention:

$$
\nabla^2u-\frac{1}{v(\mathbf r)^2}\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial t^2}
=f(\mathbf r,t),
$$

where u is scalar pressure-like amplitude, v is speed in m/s, r is distance/depth in m, t is seconds and f is a declared point-source term. For modeled gathers p_srn(v) and observations d_srn, the fitting term uses active receiver set A and frequency operator W:

$$
\Phi(v)=
\frac{\sum_{s,r\in A,n}|W[p_{sr}(v)-d_{sr}]_n|^2}
{\sum_{s,r\in A,n}|Wd_{sr,n}|^2+\epsilon}
+\beta R(v).
$$

β is a fixed dimensionless regularization weight after the code's physical scaling. R is mean squared physical velocity-gradient roughness. The code differentiates the finite-difference wave simulation and uses L-BFGS with strong-Wolfe line search. A shared data-only low-frequency 1D background precedes matched 2D full-band and multiscale schedules. Both schedules use identical observations, starting state, receiver split, model grids, prior and optimizer-call budgets; only the frozen frequency schedule differs. The withheld receiver group does not influence state selection or stopping. Every updated and terminal model is evaluated and saved.

The supplied-input path requires a closed schema with observed float32 gathers, an independently supplied starting model, exact axes/units and hashes. It calls the actual inverse without a truth argument, then exports and reopens observed, predicted, signed observed-minus-predicted residual, model and recorded states. It rejects arbitrary acquisition geometries rather than quietly approximating them.

### Assumptions, eligibility, evidence

The accepted input contract is narrow: 128 by 96 velocity mesh, 12.5 m spacing, 0.0005 s time step, configured sources at 75 m depth, and supported 20/40 receiver acquisition. The source convention and amplitude units must be declared. Engine is constant-density, scalar acoustic and 2D; it is not elastic, anisotropic, arbitrary SEG-Y or unknown-wavelet inversion. Requested CUDA must fail if unavailable, not fall back silently.

Independent validation includes directional adjoint derivative, discretization refinement, withheld receivers, malformed-input and negative failure retention, exact export reopen, and actual CUDA input/parameter effects. Full-budget external-input calculations were reported on an RTX 4070; controlled generated tests were scored against truth only after inversion/export were fixed. A finite L-BFGS budget is not a convergence certificate. Recorded work does not establish online VPS resource admission.

## M11. Gravity-magnetic structural joint inversion

### Operators and coupling

Let Gg map density contrast ρ to gravity observations dg and Gm map susceptibility χ to magnetic observations dm. In the current synthetic implementation, scaled variables are a=ρ/0.5 and b=χ/0.03, where ρ is in g/cm³ and χ is dimensionless SI. Whitened data misfit is

$$
\Phi_d(a,b)=
\operatorname{mean}\left[\left(\frac{G_g(0.5a)-d_g}{\sigma_g}\right)^2\right]
+\operatorname{mean}\left[\left(\frac{G_m(0.03b)-d_m}{\sigma_m}\right)^2\right].
$$

The structural coupling is a cross-gradient penalty

$$
\mathbf c(\mathbf r)=\nabla\rho(\mathbf r)\times\nabla\chi(\mathbf r),
\qquad
\Phi_{cg}=\lambda_{cg}\frac{\operatorname{mean}|\mathbf c|^2}
{\max(\Phi_{cg,0},\epsilon)}.
$$

The spatial finite differences use metre spacing. c has units of density contrast per metre because susceptibility is dimensionless; normalizing by the initial cross-gradient scale makes the implemented term dimensionless. A zero cross product encourages parallel gradients. It does not imply equal properties or shared causation.

The pipeline actually compares optimized uncoupled, cross-gradient coupled and Gaussian-mixture petrophysical-prior fits. The Gaussian-mixture term is the negative log density of paired scaled properties under a prior fitted in the synthetic pipeline. Separate gravity and magnetic data terms, spatial priors, optimizer histories and independent baseline ratios are retained. A structural-conflict geometry is a negative control, not a case where coupling is expected to help.

## M12. Learned first-arrival velocity inversion

### Forward operators and estimators

On a 16 by 16 grid, cell velocity vj is in m/s and slowness sj=1/vj in s/m. For ray r, A_rj is the exact path length in cell j in m. The piecewise-constant inverse predicts

$$
t_{\rm ray}=A s
$$

in seconds. Controlled observed times use a distinct quadrature operator Q over bilinearly interpolated cell-centre slowness, so the data generator does not use the exact inverse operator in heterogeneous media. This reduces one inverse-crime route but remains straight-ray physics, not a wave simulation or refracted/bent-ray oracle.

The learned model receives a coverage-normalized backprojection and normalized coverage as two grid channels and predicts bounded velocity around a depth-gradient reference. Its classical comparator estimates scaled slowness perturbation q by

$$
\widehat q=\arg\min_q\|Bq-y\|_2^2+\lambda\|Dq\|_2^2+0.01\|q\|_2^2,
$$

where B and y are scaled ray matrix and data residual, D is horizontal/vertical finite-difference roughness, and λ is selected on validation only. Both methods receive identical noisy picks and geometry. Metrics are velocity RMSE in m/s and independent-oracle travel-time RMSE in ms.

### Status and evidence

The v2 protocol generates distinct layered, lens, dipping-interface, fault and salt-like parameter families and acquisition layouts with disjoint seeds and manifests. These labels are mathematical generator names, not geological surveys. Its retained result shows learned errors substantially worse than the same-input regularized classical inverse on the joint holdout, with poor OOD detection. It fails its frozen scientific comparator and is not accepted as an inversion.

A later residual-correction v3.1 experiment was actually trained and verified. Its preregistered joint-OOD gate requires both model and independent travel-time error ratios below 1; both fail. Independent travel-time error ratio exceeds 1 in all recorded cohorts. It remains a useful failure report, not an accepted model, a field result, or evidence of production readiness. Do not make a visually plausible map a substitute for the failed physical metric.

## M13. Learned P and S earthquake phase picking

### Input, network and objective

The browser contract is a three-component trace in E/N/Z order, 60 s at 100 Hz, with tensor shape [1,3,6000]. The current STEAD model consumes unrestituted instrument counts after per-component normalization. For component c, the implementation subtracts the component mean and divides by its absolute peak using the frozen float32 order of operations. The model input is then dimensionless. Sample k represents relative time tk=0.01k s.

The implementation is an original PhaseNet-family 1D residual encoder-decoder in PyTorch, not the official PhaseNet checkpoint and not a bitwise reimplementation. It outputs logits zc,k for noise, P and S; softmax gives

$$
p_{c,k}=\frac{\exp z_{c,k}}{\sum_{j\in\{N,P,S\}}\exp z_{j,k}}.
$$

Analyst arrival labels are represented by Gaussian target curves with width σ=15 samples = 0.15 s, paired with a noise class. Weighted soft cross-entropy uses weight 12 for P and S and 1 for noise. Inference takes the global maximum of each phase curve if a development-selected threshold is met. If S is less than 40 samples (0.4 s) after P, the S pick is abstained. Model score is not calibrated uncertainty or a guarantee the analyst label is true.

Training separates events and stations across train/dev/test, quarantines cross-partition records, freezes the model and dev thresholds before opening held-out test waveforms, and compares M08 and M13 on identical IDs. Misses, false picks, timing residuals and noise/degradation strata are retained. Test waveforms are not used for threshold or checkpoint selection.

### Browser execution, rights and status

ONNX Runtime Web/WASM runs inference locally in the browser. The browser checks manifest, model, benchmark and trace SHA-256; schema, channel order, rate, length, representation, finite samples, measured channels and QC; and output finite/range/class-sum constraints. The reviewed display selection has 24 metadata-ranked entries: 23 QC-valid and one retained rejection. Rejected input is never sent to inference. A local parity receipt reports 23 valid real held-out traces with maximum probability absolute difference 4.41e-6 and zero peak-sample difference against the private frozen PyTorch oracle. This verifies numerical export parity, not arrival accuracy for those 23 examples.

The accuracy denominator is the frozen 6,000-trace test population, not the 24-trace browser display subset. ## Comparing the method boundaries

These rows solve different scientific problems. M07 uses DC resistivity and electrode geometry. M08 conditions instrument recordings and proposes classical onset times. M09 inverts first arrivals with a ray/eikonal approximation. M10 fits waveform samples through the acoustic wave equation and is more nonlinear and expensive. M11 links property structures from two physical observation operators. M12 estimates first-arrival velocity from learned synthetic priors, but the recorded comparator fails. M13 predicts phase score curves from waveform input and does not estimate velocity or event location.

A smooth image, low training misfit, cached truth, unit test, local API result or browser parity check is not a substitute for each method's eligibility conditions, independent oracle, negative controls, held-out protocol and separate release gate.
