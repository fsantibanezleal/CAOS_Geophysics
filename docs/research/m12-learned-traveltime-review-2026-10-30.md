# Learned first-arrival tomography and null-space correction: research review

Review date: 2026-10-30. Scope: the CAOS M09/M12 first-arrival methods, the proposed M12 geometry-normalized learned residual (BS-1), and the later null-space-constrained residual study. This is a focused literature review, not a systematic review: sources were selected from primary papers and publisher/arXiv records available online; no claim of exhaustive coverage is made.

## Problem classes and comparability

“Learned travel-time tomography” covers methods with materially different inputs and objectives. A supervised inverse map learns a map from measured times to a model using paired training data. A physics-informed neural network (PINN) may jointly approximate travel-time and velocity fields while minimizing pick misfit and an eikonal residual. A neural parameterization with ensemble Kalman inversion (EKI) optimizes a lower-dimensional representation and may retain an ensemble for uncertainty analysis. A post-hoc null-space correction modifies a classical estimate using a model direction that is invisible to a specified linearized acquisition operator. Results from these families cannot be ranked from separate papers with different models, acquisition, noise, forward solvers and scores.

The classical product M09 engine uses source/receiver geometry, a bounded velocity model, straight-ray path lengths and a regularized inverse with an independent LSQR comparison. That is an explicit discrete linearized approximation. Grid-based eikonal and ray-tracing methods solve different forward approximations and can change paths with the model. Product comparisons therefore need to state whether path geometry is fixed, whether the travel-time forward is linear or nonlinear, and whether the scoring operator is independent from the inversion operator.

## Primary method families

| Family | Primary source and method | Relevance and comparison boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Null-space model appraisal | Rowbotham and Pratt project prior model information into the null space of a travel-time inverse and demonstrate synthetic and field cross-hole examples. | Establishes that null-space projection in seismic travel-time inversion predates this product. It is not new in itself. [Paper, Geophysics](https://doi.org/10.1190/1.1444195). |
| Null-space exploration | Muñoz and Rath use model modification plus null-space projection to explore non-uniqueness in magnetotellurics, building on prior seismic tomography work. | Establishes a second geophysical use: model alternatives and non-uniqueness appraisal. It differs from a learned-correction objective but rules out claiming projection itself as novel. [Paper, GJI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-246X.2005.02825.x). |
| Supervised inverse map | Fan and Ying derive a convolutional structure for circular travel-time tomography and train a BCR-Net inverse map. | Learned inverse architecture is acquisition/problem specific. It is not reproduced by the CAOS checkpoint and is not a matched benchmark for the v1.1 cohorts. | Physics-informed eikonal inversion | Waheed et al. (PINNtomo) use observed travel times and a factored eikonal equation to constrain neural travel-time and velocity fields. | Physics enters training through a nonlinear PDE residual. It is conceptually distinct from conditioning an inverse CNN on path-length/backprojected features, and from post-hoc linear null-space projection. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01588). |

| Sparse-ray supervised/optimized ANN | Komeazi et al. evaluate an ANN for synthetic volcanic-edifice configurations against damped linear inversion, including sparse ray coverage and a second-order optimizer. | Directly relevant to geometry shift and sparse coverage, but still uses its own synthetic generator and cannot be ranked against the CAOS result without shared arrays and settings. | Multiparameter PINN | Song et al. extend PINNtomo to simultaneous P- and S-wave inversion and replace a source-dependent factored eikonal formulation with an effective-slowness version. | Current source-backed state of the art for multiparameter physics-informed inversion; different target and forward formulation from the single-parameter CAOS study. [Geophysical Prospecting](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2478.70034). |
| Residual PINN with multiple priors | Xiang et al. combine residual-network/SIREN parameterization with travel-time constraints, well logs and geological interfaces in an eikonal-based inversion. | A recent 2026 method with external prior information. It is not a pure-pick, same-information baseline; any benchmark must control the additional well/interface data. [Mathematics article](https://doi.org/10.3390/math14081392). |

These references show a mature landscape with inverse networks, physics-informed objectives, ensemble inference, null-space methods and data/prior fusion. They do not establish that one method is universally superior. The 2026 residual-PINN paper is especially important for avoiding an outdated “current methods” section; it is included as a literature comparator, not claimed as reproduced in CAOS.

## CAOS method and evidence

The proposed BS-1 feature method addresses a dimensional defect in an earlier learned residual input. For straight-ray matrix $A$, observed-minus-predicted time residuals have units of seconds. The physically normalized feature divides each ray residual by its path length before backprojection, then normalizes by cell coverage. The bounded residual CNN receives that field and a coverage channel. Its frozen cohorts and validation-only model selection are documented in the current SDD and `docs/design/features/velocity-physics-refinement/`. The completed GPU study selected epoch 40, but the learned model lost to the matched classical comparator and flagged all 160 joint-shift examples. This is a negative result for the proposed BS-1 hypothesis, not evidence of novelty or advantage.

The later null-space experiment starts from a retained learned velocity prediction $v_\ell$ and classical velocity $v_c$. In slowness, $\delta s=1/v_\ell-1/v_c$. For an orthonormal basis $N$ of $\ker(A)$, it constructs

$$s_\alpha=\operatorname{clip}(1/v_c+\alpha NN^\mathsf{T}\delta s,1/4000,1/1400),\qquad v_\alpha=1/s_\alpha.$$

The correction scale is chosen on calibration data only and is admissible only when both velocity RMSE and independent travel-time RMSE improve. A zero-scale fallback returns the classical model. The protocol evaluates 160 calibration realizations and four disjoint test cohorts totaling 480 cases. The frozen full receipt supports a bounded synthetic result; a float-safe wrapper changes only the tolerance for recomputed candidate-score mapping equality and delegates all remaining checks to the original verifier.

The corrected paired-bootstrap analysis resamples case identifiers, then computes the ratio of mean per-case RMSEs for 20,000 NumPy PCG64 draws (seed 6122026). In the joint family/acquisition shift, velocity RMSE ratio is 0.9701 with 95% interval 0.9661–0.9737; independent bilinear travel-time RMSE ratio is 0.9967 with interval 0.9950–0.9983. The intervals quantify resampling variation inside those fixed generated cohorts only. The original scorer passed an infinite threshold, so the zero-threshold-exceedance fields in that artifact are not valid threshold evidence and are excluded.

## Novelty and inference assessment

The null-space operation is established and the product experiment does not compare it with Rowbotham–Pratt’s prior-informed construction, Muñoz–Rath’s non-uniqueness exploration, PINNtomo, EKI-NN, the sparse-ray ANN or current residual PINNs on matched arrays. More importantly, no unprojected prediction from the inherited checkpoint is saved for the exact four follow-up test cohorts. Therefore the measured difference against the classical baseline does not isolate the incremental causal effect of projection. The independent bilinear travel-time operator is a useful scoring control, but it does not replace a shared benchmark.

The narrow conclusion is that this particular projected candidate had lower aggregate model and independent travel-time RMSE than the classical baseline in four fixed synthetic cohorts. The joint-shift differences are small, particularly for travel time. The experiment does not establish geological uniqueness, resolution, uncertainty calibration, external reproducibility, performance on field data, or superiority to published state-of-the-art methods. No global novelty claim is supported. The working manuscript `manuscripts/m12-nullspace-refinement-v0.01/main.tex` follows this boundary.

## Work needed for a stronger scientific claim

1. Recompute and retain the raw learned prediction, classical model, projected model and independent predictions on identical new calibration/test seeds. Compare all three, not only projected versus classical.
2. Include matched controls for unprojected correction, a classical regularized null-space/prior correction, and a no-learned-information projection. Freeze all tuning on calibration data.
3. Replace a dense full SVD with a scalable, tolerance-documented null-space or projected-gradient method before increasing grid size; report conditioning and projection error versus computational cost.
4. Add nonlinear eikonal/fast-marching evaluation, realistic pick errors and source/receiver uncertainty; distinguish linearized null-space preservation from nonlinear travel-time preservation after clipping and velocity conversion.
5. Evaluate multiple independent geological generators, acquisition geometries, noise and model misspecification, with a second-group reproduction and an eligible field dataset before claiming transfer.
6. Compare against current methods using common inputs and information. PINN, EKI and prior-guided systems must not receive extra information without a separate information-matched arm.

Until those studies exist, retain the result as a bounded research branch and keep M12 scientific acceptance false. The current evidence is appropriate for a working manuscript that reports the method and its limits; it is not a publishable superiority claim.
